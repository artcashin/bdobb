BDOBB · app icon candidates

Four directions blending the chart/market and LED/retro-terminal briefs — LED green #3dff8a and accent blue #4f8cc9 on the app's near-black navy, each with a restrained glow. Every candidate is a full-bleed square master, shown here through each platform's corner mask and at taskbar sizes.

The brief: works on Windows, macOS and iPadOS  ·  direction: chart/market + LED terminal  ·  palette: green + blue together  ·  tone: mostly flat with a little atmosphere.

A · “Segment B”

A glowing LED-segment B — the Clock widget's typeface spirit — riding above a blue ticker tape.

Why it works. This is the monogram play, and it earns its place because the letterform is drawn in the app's own voice: the broken-segment strokes and green glow come straight from the Erbos Draco clock and the tape-in-the-closet heritage. It has the strongest silhouette of the four — at 16px in a Windows taskbar it's simply a bright green B, which is exactly what you want an icon to collapse to. The blue tape underneath gives it the finance context at larger sizes without ever being load-bearing.

Watch out. Monograms are common in docks (Bear, Bloomberg's own B). The LED treatment differentiates it, but if you sit it next to other letter-icons it competes on typography rather than imagery. The ticker line disappears below 32px — by design, but worth knowing.

B · “The Tape”

Three rising candlesticks — blue structure, green signal — with a glowing trend line cutting through.

Why it works. The most literal candidate: nobody mistakes what this app does. The blue-to-green progression (two blue candles resolving into one glowing green one) quietly tells the up-and-to-the-right story, and the candle motif scales beautifully — at 16px it degrades into three colored bars, which still reads as "markets." This is the safest choice for discoverability: on an iPad home screen full of icons, it self-describes.

Watch out. It's also the most generic direction — hundreds of trading apps use candlesticks. The specific color pairing and glow keep it from looking like stock clip-art, but it says "a finance app" more than it says "BDOBB." No letterform means the brand rides entirely on color.

C · “Data-bar B”

The letter B constructed from horizontal data bars, cooling from LED green at the top to accent blue at the base.

Why it works. The hybrid: a monogram that is literally made of chart material. The bar construction reads as an order book or equalizer at large sizes and fuses into a solid B at small ones — a nice "the closer you look, the more data you see" property that suits an app whose whole point is composing widgets into a desktop. The green-to-blue gradient is the only candidate that makes both brand colors structural rather than decorative.

Watch out. It's the busiest mark. At 16px the bars fuse and it can flirt with reading as an 8 rather than a B — the enlarged 16px sample below is the honest test. If you love the direction, a refinement pass on the bowl gaps would tighten it further.

D · “The Widget”

A blue widget-card frame holding a green LED stairstep sparkline — the app's own dashboard card, distilled.

Why it works. The most "product-true" candidate: it's not a metaphor for finance in general, it's a picture of what BDOBB actually is — a card, on your desktop, with live green data in it. The stairstep line is deliberately digital (segment-display geometry, square endpoint pixel) so the LED heritage shows without needing type. The blue frame / green data split gives each brand color a clear job, and the composition is unique enough that nothing else in a dock resembles it.

Watch out. At 16px the frame thins and the mark leans on the green staircase alone — still legible, but the "card" idea only fully lands at 32px and up. The framed-rectangle motif also echoes generic "app window" icons; the LED staircase is what keeps it specific.

My recommendation

First choice: D, “The Widget.” It's the only candidate that describes this app rather than finance apps in general — a widget card with live data glowing inside it is BDOBB's entire thesis in one picture. It uses both brand colors structurally, carries the LED heritage in the stairstep geometry, and nothing else in a dock looks like it.

Close second: A, “Segment B.” The strongest small-size silhouette and the deepest tie to the series' personality (the Clock face, the tape). If you expect BDOBB to live mostly in a crowded taskbar at tiny sizes, A's instant legibility is a real argument for it.

B is the safe, self-describing choice if you'd rather be found than be distinctive; C is the most conceptually interesting but needs one more refinement pass to fully escape the “8 vs B” ambiguity at 16px.

Production notes (when you pick one)

Each master is a 1024×1024 SVG with the mark held inside a ~78% safe zone, so macOS/iPadOS corner masking never clips it. From a 1024px PNG export, pnpm tauri icon icon.png generates the whole set for you: icon.icns (macOS), icon.ico (Windows, 16–256px), the PNG ladder for Linux, and the iOS AppIcon set your Ep. 7 iPad build needs (iPadOS requires a fully opaque icon — these are, by design). For Windows I'd additionally hand-check the generated 16px slice; if it muddies, we swap in a simplified small-size variant into the .ico, which ICO files support per-size.
